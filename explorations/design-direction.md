# Howl design direction

**Chosen direction: Round 02, version B — Open Line.** It keeps the dark, quick-moving feed while giving posts room to read. Howl should feel loud, warm and unmistakably made for NC State students—not like a bank app. Campus life comes through in students’ words and energy, not campus illustrations.

## Look and feel

- **Color:** near-black `#0B0B0B` background; `#F4F4F4` main text; `#A0A0A0` secondary text; `#343434` dividers; `#171717` secondary surfaces; Howl red `#CC0000` for the wordmark and primary actions. Keep B’s palette consistent.
- **Type:** Arial throughout. Set post text at 18px with 1.65 line-height; keep metadata smaller and quiet. Use clear, restrained weight and size differences rather than decorative type.
- **Shapes:** simple, mostly square-edged surfaces separated by thin rules. Use small round avatars. Keep the red wordmark and action buttons crisp; avoid card stacks, shadows and decorative effects.
- **Layout and rhythm:** prioritize the feed. On phones, use at least 16px screen-edge space and readable rows; on laptops, use left navigation, a centered feed and a quiet right rail. Use the spacing set of 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64px.

## Do

1. Let real student posts lead, with readable 18px text and comfortable line spacing.
2. Keep the near-black, off-white, gray and Howl-red palette consistent.
3. Use thin dividers, small round avatars and simple edges to organize the feed.
4. Make controls clear, keyboard-visible and large enough to tap; show their state.
5. Adapt the feed for phones and laptops instead of stretching one layout.

## Don’t

1. Don’t turn Howl into a polished finance dashboard or a generic social template.
2. Don’t add gradients, glow, glass, neon, decorative blobs or campus-themed illustrations.
3. Don’t shrink post text or crowd rows until the feed is hard to read.
4. Don’t put posts in identical rounded cards or use shadows as decoration.
5. Don’t use emoji as icons, filler labels, made-up metrics or numbering that adds no meaning.
